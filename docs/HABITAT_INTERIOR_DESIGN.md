# Selected compact two-disc habitat

The ship retains two counter-rotating inflatable discs, each 19.5 m radius and
4.5 m axial depth, with floor radii 17.5/13.5/9.5 m. At 0.5g on the outer floor,
spin is about 5.05 rpm; inner floors have approximately 0.386g and 0.271g.
Vestibular adaptation, gravity conversion under thrust and medical recovery remain
unqualified. The full cruise spine and engine standoff remain pending radiation analysis; flight-only structure and spin bearings are detached in orbit before descent. Pressure/service hubs and deployable frames remain for settlement.

Each disc has eight 12 m2 shared cabins for sixteen people, arranged in four
residential groups. Each couple has two medical beds that also serve as regular
beds. Both partners wake together. Sixteen couples stagger starts every 42 hours,
so four pairs are in their awake week throughout the 7/21 cycle.

The per-disc programme is 484 m2 within approximately 496 m2 net area at the
assumed 65% net factor. This is a compact area study, not proof of a globally
minimum ship or a qualified human-factors fit.

Each deck is a ring of a few large rooms rather than twelve separate bays, with no
continuous corridor. The outer deck alternates four private cabin pods (two closed
couple cabins each) with four communal rooms: dining and hygiene, health and exercise,
recovery and airlock, counselling and pantry. The middle and inner decks each hold four
90-degree rooms. Fittings fill most of the 4.5 m axial depth; the remainder is a single
1.2 m aisle inside each room. Doorways in bulkheads let people walk through the rooms in
sequence. Four ladder shafts per disc run through every deck to the hub, one in each
communal room. Each cabin has its own porthole, with beds along the side partitions.

In cruise the two discs are coaxial and counter-rotating, one either side of the 4 m
spine frame with 0.34 m clearance, so their angular momentum cancels and the habitat stack
is only 14 m deep. Habitat B is turned to mirror A, with its windows outboard. The hubs are
joined by a straight, non-rotating 5 m transfer corridor through the spin bearings inside
the frame; the crew float between habitats. The frontal cross-section is set by the disc
diameter (40 m) and the 15.5 m magnetic-nozzle ring, so the dust shield is 40 x 15.5 m
(31 t, previously 40 x 20 m). The refuge and rover ride above the spine aft of the discs.
The cruise vehicle is about 163 m long. Touchdown and settlement layouts are unchanged.

The outer deck includes cabins, dining, hygiene, clinical recovery and exercise.
The middle deck provides lounges, quiet library, arts/music, a second dining room,
training, laboratory, mission control, private calls and water/life support.
The inner deck carries small fresh-food gardens, maintainable plant, clean workshop,
stores and tools. Sixteen dining places per disc support all-awake operations.

Equipment geometry follows recognisable spacecraft equipment: rack backplanes,
filter columns, pumps, valves, canisters, gloveboxes, exercise devices, galley
fixtures, hygiene units and service lines. ISS reference informs equipment shape;
it does not validate this rotating inflatable structure. Floors and cabin plates
are checked for overlap, but access, egress, fire safety and pressure compartments
still require engineering.

Two rigid service modules house isolated food-production trains, independent of
habitat rotation; both remain useful after landing. Four internal garden zones
provide 60 m2 total fresh-food canopy. Large surface greenhouses shown in settlement
are future local construction, not concealed carried mass.

For landing the membranes deflate, floors fold and rigid equipment retains its
full dimensions in covered racks. The crew occupy a 32-seat rigid refuge, 5.2 m
diameter and 12 m long. The two discs and their contents deploy again on the surface.
A cruise pressure module becomes the two-person/seven-day rover cabin; it is not
counted twice. Deployment hardware, pressure packing, foundations and the 20-year
material life remain unqualified.

Right-drag pans at the clicked surface depth, left-drag orbits, scroll zooms.
Spin runs on cached GPU geometry. Ten main views prepare in a background worker;
cutaway, cover and exterior configurations retain the same rigid inventory.

The 31 t forward shield, main cruise engine, bulk antimatter store and excess hot
radiators do not descend. A dedicated direct/thermal engine, local containment and
required hot cooling deliver the settlement; all engine/staging targets are unqualified.

## Engine burns (1.2 g along the ship)

During each 63-day burn the discs are locked and thrust gravity points aft. Each room's folding
floor panels re-set at right angles to the thrust, turning the room into a stack of short levels
(about 2-3 m long by 4.5 m deep, 2.1 m clear height) joined by vertical or inclined ladders. Cabins
take 2.6-2.9 m slots on their pod's levels, widened across the disc to keep about 12 m2 each.
Eight carbon burn braces run from rim hardpoints to spine nodes clear of the rims, so each disc's
inertial load (roughly 55-60 t, about 0.7 MN at 1.2 g, so about 170 kN per brace) does not rest on
the hub bearing alone. The braces are retracted while the discs spin. Their mass is not yet in the
budget. The viewer's Engine mode shows the antimatter exhaust as a long glowing column.

## Settlement on Proxima b

A thin, dusty atmosphere is assumed for surface operations and the view (the real atmosphere is
unknown; the descent mass model still ignores it, which is conservative for propellant). Proxima
spans about 1.7 degrees in the sky and Alpha Centauri A and B appear as a bright close pair near
magnitudes -6.6 and -5.3. Habitat A, habitat B and the greenhouse are joined by pressurised
corridors at the ring floor height, so the whole settlement can be walked internally. The eight
greenhouse halls are glazed vaults on foundations either side of a corridor spine. The power island
is a regolith-bermed 25 MW-thermal antimatter converter (10 MWe working load) about 190 m from the
nearest habitat, with about 2,300 m2 of 600 K radiators, a switchyard, critical-load solar backup,
an exclusion fence and a buried cable to a substation by the habitat link. The standoff is a design
value; a dose analysis is still required.
