# Family fleet redesign specification — concept v1

**Exposure audit:** [COSMIC_RAY_EXPOSURE.md](COSMIC_RAY_EXPOSURE.md) quantifies
published-rate screening cases and the user's concentrated-torpor-shielding
priority. Current radiation performance does not meet the target in those
benchmark cases. The refuge's 20.45 g/cm2 figure below is a thin-layer inventory
screen; a finite uniform shell from 44 t is about 19.32 cm water, not 20 cm.

12 November 2026. Selected conceptual architecture for a later visual redesign.
This supersedes competing geometry and allocation suggestions in the earlier
planning studies. It does not change the current viewer or active input files.

Machine-readable specification: [specs/redesign_v1.json](specs/redesign_v1.json).
Reproducible ledger: [redesign-v1.json](../reports/redesign-v1.json), generated by

## 1. What is selected, and what is not solved

Select a 40-year family crew vehicle plus a 60-year narrow cargo precursor.
Cargo departs 20 years before crew; both arrive at fleet year 60. Reserve the
last 0.1 year of each flight for arrival operations. The main transfers are
therefore 39.9 and 59.9 years. Births occur approximately around crew years
18–22 using stored embryos; capacity is 32 original adults and 32 children.

Select three compact pressure modules, two rotating and one stationary; useful
water/fuel cells; a local all-crew refuge; twin remote towing propulsion pods;
main-engine approach braking; an arrival-only expandable aeroshell; and chemical
engines for the final descent. Land the three habitat modules and essential
survival/exploration systems; leave the interstellar propulsion assembly in a
controlled orbit. Cargo carries heavy surface industry and expansion equipment.

This is a concrete design-to specification, not a flight-qualified solution.
In particular, there is no demonstrated lightweight system closing forty years
of GCR exposure. That requirement remains explicitly open, with a 30 t hardware
allowance that may prove insufficient. It is not silently considered solved by
a water column or a decorative magnetic field. The relativistic engine, long
storage life, structural allocations, food closure and entry trajectory also
require validation. A visual implementation may depict the concept, but must
not label it safe, feasible, minimum-mass or engineering-complete.

## 2. Principal dimensions and accommodation

Coordinate system: metres; z longitudinal, negative z forward. Origin at the
common module centre. The habitation bus keeps its nose toward incoming gas
throughout acceleration, coast and braking.

| Element | Centre | Dimensions / state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gravity module A | (0,0,-16) | Sphere, 800 m3, radius 5.759 m |
| Common/services module | (0,0,0) | Sphere, 800 m3, stationary |
| Gravity module B | (0,0,+16) | Sphere, 800 m3, opposite spin |
| Forward shield | (0,0,-35) | 14 m diameter; independent sacrificial face and load-bearing backing |
| Refuge inside common module | (0,0,0) | 6.4 m diameter, 7.5 m long; 241 m3 included in the 800 m3 |
| Entry aeroshell | Near forward backing | 50 m deployed diameter; stowed throughout cruise |
| Towing engines, acceleration | (+/-100,0,-2000) | Two pods, connected by a 200 m compression crossbeam |
| Towing engines, braking | (+/-100,0,+2000) | Same assembly repositioned with engines off |

Total pressure volume is 2,400 m3; total ideal spherical envelope area is about
1,250 m2. Intermodule transfers use the roughly 4.48 m axial gaps. Do not count
the refuge or corridors twice in the volume display. Tanks and reaction-mass
storage extend axially behind the habitation bus inside the forward footprint.
Their final length is derived from the calculated inventory, not an arbitrary
short decorative tank. Distributed protection cells are at most 0.25 m deep;
surplus water stays in remote storage and replenishes cells.

Two cylindrical internal floor surfaces per gravity sphere, radii 5.0 and 2.8 m,
provide about 356 m2 gross floor area per module. The 2.2 m radial separation
avoids the inadequate headroom of closely spaced concentric decks. At about
11.96 rpm the outer floor has 0.8g and the inner floor 0.448g. These values are
explicit visual/design parameters under the user's accepted gravity premise;
they are not a demonstrated human-factors prescription. They can change after
layout/rotation qualification without silently changing the volume requirement.

Each gravity module houses 16 original adults and 16 children. Target per module:

| Function | Gross allocated floor area |
| --- | ---: |
| Sixteen double cabins, 32 convertible sleeping places | 96 m2 |
| School/play | 50 m2 |
| Dining/lounge | 40 m2 |
| Hygiene | 20 m2 |
| Exercise | 25 m2 |
| Service/access equipment | 30 m2 |
| Circulation | 70 m2 |
| Remaining gross allowance | About 25 m2 |

These areas are packing targets. Verify real cabin headroom, curvature, doors,
egress and equipment access before treating them as usable floor area.
Sleeping and medical/torpor places use the same 64 convertible berths; they are
not 128 beds. Normal torpor is in the gravity modules. All 64 can be awake for
emergencies and arrival. Adult-only nominal roster is eight awake; the family
phase provides approximately sixteen awake adults plus all awake children.
Caregiving coverage must be demonstrated; eight adults is not the family cap.

Common-module gross space screen: 241 m3 refuge/medical core, 180 m3 food and
life-support processing, 120 m3 workshop/stores, 180 m3 common recreation and
79 m3 transfer/access provision. Functions may overlap operationally, not in
the volume ledger. The refuge is for emergencies, not twenty years of living.

Use sealed transfer capsules: synchronise with a rotating module, hard-dock,
transfer, close hatches, undock, despin and hard-dock to the static module. A
1.8 m internal-diameter, 2.6 m long capsule is the dimensional starting point;
one per gravity module plus a stowed spare is the operating concept. No credit
is taken for a decades-long continuously sliding pressure seal. Accessibility,
litter transfer, throughput, failure recovery and emergency refuge access are
qualification gates. Do not assume stellar events always provide enough warning
to transfer all torpid occupants; local cell protection and occupancy procedures
must cover that case.

## 3. Habitation construction target

Select hybrid composite restraint shells/cores with replaceable gas liners,
insulation and non-forward impact layers. Allow expandable packing where it
saves transport or surface-deployment volume, but do not require all modules
to be inflatable. Compare actual load cases before final material selection.

Envelope/floors target: 30 t before allowance, initially divided into 4 t primary
restraint, 2 t liners, 6 t insulation/local impact protection, 12 t floors, and
6 t frames/hatches/reinforcement. Spin/transfer systems add 12 t and furnishings
12 t. The resulting basic accommodation target is **67.5 t including 25% allowance**.
It is an aggressive bill-of-functions target, not a demonstrated build mass.

Pressure, creep/fatigue, spin loads, concentrated tanks, engine acceleration,
entry/landing loads and fire/compartment isolation must all be sized. Do not use
the ideal 1–5 t pressure-membrane screen as the complete habitat mass. No cut
to life support, medical redundancy or growth accommodation is justified solely
by reducing envelope surface area.

## 4. Propulsion layout and braking reconfiguration

Main engine remains an explicitly hypothetical annihilation-powered massive
exhaust system: reference 50% energy into directed reaction-mass kinetic energy,
1 GW/kg handled annihilation power, 5% ordinary tank mass coefficient, and
1 kg scalable containment per kg antimatter. The optimiser selects exhaust
speed and finite burn duration for lowest antimatter within this model.
Keep the 5% main propellant reserve. The 12 km/s approach/correction allocation
is drawn from that reserve; the small-velocity calculation does not create a
second uncounted fuel supply.

Use two towing pods 2 km from the occupied bus, displaced 100 m to either side.
Main reaction material is ordinary water in bus tanks; insulated feeds follow
the tow paths. Antimatter storage stays in remote pods. Each pod has two
independent load paths, each sized for full pod tow load. A 200 m compression
crossbeam is mandatory: without it, the inward cable force would require thrust
directed away from the bus and exhaust pointed back toward it. Crossbeam mass,
reels, tethers, deployment and feeds receive 27 t before allowance.

At the reference optimum the first cable-strength screen is approximately
7.3 t for four 2 km paths at an assumed 1 GPa allowable and 1,600 kg/m3 density.
Crossbeam compression is about 28 kN. These screens omit creep, erosion, dynamics,
redundancy beyond the defined paths and local fittings; remaining allocation is
not evidence those requirements close.

Require the primary directed exhaust to remain within a 1-degree half-angle.
At the bus this gives about 35 m plume radius and about 58 m geometric clearance
to the 7 m protection envelope. This is a requirement, not demonstrated nozzle
performance. Scattered particles and radiation still require transport analysis.

Before braking, shut down propulsion, despin/reconfigure service equipment as
needed, reposition the assembly to z=+2000, change from fore to aft anchors,
and re-establish tension before thrust. Keep the main bus and forward dust shield
facing incoming material. The tugs have their own small forward impact protection.
Reconfiguration propulsion, feed handling and clearance must be analysed; no
instantaneous 180-degree flip or exposed crew nose is implied.

## 5. Separate protection systems

### Forward impacts

Use a 14 m sacrificial layered front shield with a replaceable exposed face,
standoff from pressure shells, and a secondary-particle layer oriented toward
the bus. The current mass screen is 100 kg/m2 plus 3 t support; this is not a
qualified stopping/erosion specification. A route gas/dust spectrum and rare
grain damage criterion are required. Thin tethers/crossbeam/pod fronts outside
the main disc need their own erosion analysis; they are not protected merely
because the space between them is empty.

### Engine radiation

Put a local shadow shield 50 m from each engine source on the crew-facing line.
Start with 0.8 m radius, 0.5 m tungsten thickness per shield, plus 4 t combined
neutron layers/mounts. Calculated tungsten mass is about 38.8 t for the pair;
the package is about 42.8 t before allowance. A 0.5 m effective source radius
and 7 m occupied footprint at 2 km require approximately 0.663 m geometric shadow
radius, leaving geometric margin. Actual source dimensions/secondary emissions
may invalidate this construction.

The shadow shields intercept energy and need cooling. Using a 38% prompt-gamma
fraction and full deposition, add **2.432e-5** of total annihilation power to
the intrinsic engine heat fraction; do not hide it within the previous 1e-5.
The model sizes hot radiators for the combined load. This primary-ray/thermal
screen is not a radiation-dose solution: neutron production, buildup, muons,
finite exhaust decay regions, shield erosion and penetrations remain gates.

### Stellar events and working inventory

Place 36 t protected retained water plus the 8 t working-water inventory around
the central 241 m3 refuge. Its 215 m2 envelope receives approximately 205 kg/m2
if all 44 t is in the allocated wall positions. Working-water outages require
alternate cells or occupancy procedures; no gap may be concealed by an average.
That approximately 20 g/cm2 column is a storm-shelter design screen, not lifetime
GCR protection. Retained 36 t is not expendable main reserve. It lands with the
habitat and can become surface water/storage shielding. The earlier 88 t extra
water and 159 t hydrogen annulus are not included.

### Galactic cosmic rays: unresolved performance requirement

Use ordinary reaction material in distributed cells around the gravity cabins,
medical areas and high-occupancy common spaces. Drain remote stores first.
Report actual coverage through the full burn schedule; do not maintain an
arbitrary 20 g/cm2 over every room by adding unspecified ballast.

Design target for original adults: no more than 400 mSv over transit, leaving
part of a 600 mSv adult reference for other lifetime exposure. Allocate 10 mSv
engine, 50 mSv forward secondaries, 40 mSv stellar events and 300 mSv GCR/service
power. These are engineering targets, not approved medical limits. Children and
pregnancy need their own organ/age-dependent limits, not an adult dose allowance.

A 30 t GCR mitigation hardware allowance is reserved, including possible active
systems and local additional material. There is no proven design meeting that
target at that mass. NIAC magnetic-shield concepts are research leads; they do
not demonstrate a safe forty-year mission. In the viewer this is a labelled
reserved subsystem, not an invented functioning magnetic bubble. If dose/transport
analysis cannot close, this concept cannot be described as a viable human mission.

## 6. Service power, food and exploration

Select two independent 500 kWe fission-electric service cassettes, nominal and
reserve, with 15 t hardware target each plus 1 t total fuel inventory. This removes
continuous antimatter power generation close to the occupied habitat. Reactors
are remote on the tow assembly in transit, return in shielded casks for entry,
and are deployed remotely on the surface. Carry and shield shutdown decay heat
and radiation; shutdown alone is not safe handling. Transport/cask/shielding and
40-year life must close within or revise these targets.

Nominal service load target: 500 kWe. Preliminary allocation: 200 kW food/LSS,
100 kW thermal/cryogenic services, 70 kW habitation/medical, 40 kW pumps/controls,
20 kW communications and 70 kW operating headroom. This is a load allocation,
not measured plant performance. High transient loads must be buffered and
sequenced; a reactor failure cannot take both full-population food trains offline.

Food/LSS 24 t, medical and convertible berths 18 t, exploration/tools/suits 10 t.
Retain two complete food-production trains, protected cultures and independent
critical recovery loops. Closed material loops over decades remain an enabling
assumption. Inventory is 75 t in the 40-year case: 22 t fixed people/growth/loop
stocks, 10 t adult-duration maintenance, 6 t family-phase maintenance, 36 t refuge
water and 1 t reactor fuel. The existing 8 t process water is within the fixed
22 t, not added twice. No child-torpor nutritional credit is taken.

Crew exploration allocation: a small utility rover, robotic scouts, four suit
systems, geological/biological instruments, common repair equipment and spares.
Cargo delivers heavy surface industry and larger expansion equipment. Do not
make independent crew survival contingent on successful cargo landing.

## 7. Arrival and landing

Baseline is explicitly conditional on a usable atmosphere. Do not assume a
confirmed Mars-like atmosphere or promise the same light lander works in vacuum.
Confirm the atmosphere/entry corridor before committing to this arrival mode.
Cargo launch twenty years early does not establish this at crew launch: it is
still in transit. Earlier reconnaissance/remote observations or acceptance of
an orbital-only contingency are required before committing people.

Sequence:

1. Complete interstellar braking with nose protection maintained.
2. Use main propulsion for suitable approach/capture maneuvers; do not hover.
3. Survey atmospheric profile, terrain and entry corridor; prepare contingency orbit.
4. Transfer service-power cassettes to the descent cradle, stop/lock habitat
   rotation, restrain all 64 people and isolate propulsion interfaces.
5. Put nonlanding propulsion equipment into a controlled orbit. Do not drop an
   active stage kilometres above the landing site.
6. Deploy a **50 m aeroshell** on the existing forward backing. Use a fresh TPS
   face; the eroded interstellar surface is not assumed entry-qualified.
7. Enter under a validated trajectory, then use four 3 MN hydrolox engines for
   terminal braking and landing. One failed engine leaves 9 MN.
8. Deploy surface supports, separate/arrange the three habitat modules, transfer
   retained water, and establish local surface shielding before prolonged exposure.

Chemical terminal engine baseline: 450 s Isp, 1 km/s allowance, 10% propellant
reserve and tank coefficient 8%. Main-engine fuel for 12 km/s approach is small
and drawn from the main correction reserve. It does not eliminate terminal
high-thrust needs or gravity losses.

Calculated crew descent load is approximately 457 t at terminal start, including
about **100 t total chemical propellant**, not 100 t hydrogen. At oxygen/hydrogen
mass ratio 6, this is approximately **14.3 t hydrogen and 85.7 t oxygen**. This
cuts the hydrogen storage requirement sharply while avoiding a local annihilation
source under the lander. The chemical fuel is not automatically credited as a
uniform cosmic-ray shield.

Handover design gate: at most 400 m/s downward speed at at least 12 km altitude.
With constant initial mass and one engine failed, the vertical screen gives
about 10 km stopping distance and 982 m/s propulsive delta-v. Nominal four-engine
performance provides more headroom; drag and falling mass change the trajectory.
This is a feasibility screen, NOT evidence that the atmosphere/aeroshell delivers
those initial conditions. Aerodynamic entry, terrain, winds, controllability,
loads, heat and divert margins must be simulated. No altitude is radiation-qualified
for direct antimatter exhaust, hence the chemical terminal choice.

Deployable aeroshell/fresh TPS target is 12 t before allowance. That is a mass
allocation, not a demonstrated 50 m shield. Actual sizing can change the budget.

Alternative branches, not automatic baseline savings:

- If an entry simulation supports 0.5 km/s chemical terminal allowance, crew
  terminal propellant becomes about 46.6 t; crew main antimatter about 41.5 t.
- A qualified 1,800 s antimatter-heated terminal system could reduce terminal
  hydrogen to about 22.5 t and crew main antimatter to about 39.5 t, holding other
  hardware fixed for comparison. Its radiation/heater/thrust requirements are
  not solved by the comparison; chemical hardware cannot simply be relabelled.
- An airless 6 km/s chemical branch needs roughly 1,492 t terminal propellant
  for the unchanged landed hardware. It is not the baseline and shows why a
  vacuum variant requires a new stage/arrival design before departure.

## 8. Cargo definition

Uncrewed 60-year flight, 6 m forward shield, 4 m diameter ordinary-water tank
modules stacked along the axis. Use calculated inventory to derive cylindrical
lengths and endcaps. Two propulsion pods are offset +/-20 m and 200 m from cargo,
with a 40 m crossbeam and 10 t tow-assembly target. No crew shadow shields or
human GCR allowance are charged to cargo; electronics and source controls still
need local radiation protection. No crew wheel or full habitat is added.

Surface manifest: 30 t industry/resource processing, 10 t heavy exploration,
15 t habitat expansion, 15 t food/LSS backup and 15 t power/services. These are
hardware targets before allowance. Stocks and maintenance add 26.5 t. Baseline
landing uses a 35 m aeroshell and four 1.5 MN chemical engines, with a 1 km/s
terminal allowance. Entry must be qualified separately from the crew vehicle.

## 9. Closed concept ledger

All hardware carries a 25% allowance. Main fuel, tanks, engine power, containment
and heat-rejection feedback are solved together. Fission service fuel and landing
propellants are counted once. Dry hardware includes the unresolved GCR allowance;
it is not evidence of a completed GCR subsystem.

| Reference engine assumptions | Crew | Cargo | Fleet |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Dry hardware | 693 t | 249 t | 943 t |
| Loaded departure | 2,670 t | 979 t | 3,649 t |
| Loaded antimatter | 46.0 t | 6.9 t | **52.9 t** |
| Terminal chemical propellant | 99.9 t | 46.6 t | 146.5 t |
| Touchdown mass excluding unused terminal propellant | 357 t | 166 t | 524 t |

Antimatter and matching ordinary annihilation matter are already inside loaded
departure mass. The 12 km/s approach allowance comes from the main reserve;
nominal trajectory results are not additional zero-cost maneuvers. The main
reference crew burns last about 2.55 coordinate years each, peaking at about
0.1135c. Cargo burns last about 1.97 years each and peak near 0.0732c.

Optimistic propulsion assumptions produce about 526 t crew dry, 214 t cargo
dry, 3,293 t combined departure and 24.2 t fleet antimatter. These are a technology
sensitivity, not an assured lower bound. The reference dry result is higher
than the prior 614 t allocation because this version charges explicitly for
shadow shielding, tow geometry, service reactors and chemical terminal engines.
That is an accounting correction, not a demonstrated increase in physical minimum.

Carry **55 t fleet antimatter and 3,700 t fleet departure** as rounded provisional
planning figures for this particular concept. Do not describe that rounding as
a certified contingency margin. If the unresolved GCR/engine/EDL requirements
need more hardware, these figures must increase or the architecture must change.

## 10. Visual implementation contract

Use the stable IDs and metre coordinates in the JSON. Do not overwrite the old
ship data in place: introduce concept v1 as a separately selectable proposal.

- Crew detail view shows three modules, counterrotation, internal decks/cabins,
  static common module, refuge, useful-water cells and forward shield.
- Full-scale view shows the 2 km separation, outboard pods, compression crossbeam,
  paired tow paths, main feeds and source shadow shields. Include a scale bar.
- Cargo view is a narrow axial stack with its own short tow assembly and no
  copied family accommodation.
- Phase selector: acceleration, coast, family phase, braking reconfiguration,
  braking, orbit/staging, entry, terminal descent and surface deployment.
- During braking move the engine assembly behind the bus; retain nose/shield
  attitude. Do not animate a flip that exposes the occupied vehicle.
- During entry show the actual 50/35 m aeroshells and four terminal engines;
  remove orbital main-stage hardware, lock rotation and retain the shield backing.
- During surface deployment retain three habitation modules, water inventory,
  medical/food facilities and exploration; remotely deploy power cassettes.
- Mass overlay distinguishes dry hardware, ordinary propellant, antimatter,
  matching annihilation matter, required inventories and unresolved allowances.
- Radiation overlays distinguish forward impact shadow, engine shadow, local
  refuge and distributed working-mass protection. Never render a universal
  guaranteed-safe bubble. GCR status is prominently UNRESOLVED.

Acceptance checks before visual implementation is called complete: dimensions
and volumes reconcile; no double-counted core or beds; both departure/arrival
timelines match; all 64 have assigned spaces/restraints; mass totals match JSON;
staging removes only the selected components; source/crew/plume geometry remains
consistent across phases; assumptions and unresolved gates are visible.

## 11. Engineering gates before any feasibility claim

1. Particle transport closes GCR, children/pregnancy, engine, forward-secondary
   and stellar-event exposure; verify shelter access, fuel depletion and leaks.
2. Qualify annihilation conversion, exhaust collimation, storage, specific power,
   finite source size, shadow shielding and heat rejection together.
3. Close structural/thermal mass for pressure modules, floors, tanks, 2 km tethers,
   crossbeam, transfer capsules and rotating/entry load cases.
4. Simulate the actual atmospheric profile, capture/entry corridor, 50 m aeroshell,
   one-engine-out descent, abort orbit and stage disposal. Choose a vacuum variant
   before departure if the atmosphere cannot support the selected landing mode.
5. Demonstrate long-life food/material loops, power/reactor shielding and handling,
   torpor/medical operations, spares and human-factors performance.

These gates distinguish a concrete concept specification from a flight design.
No currently retrieved research establishes all of them for this mission.
